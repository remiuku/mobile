# Architecture

The data flows through these layers:

1. **Session tracking**: `playback/ExternalPlaybackViewModel.kt`. `SessionAccessService` is an empty `NotificationListenerService` that exists only to grant `MediaSessionManager` access. The VM watches all active sessions, prefers a playing local player over a remote mirror, and keeps the lyric clock in `LyricClock` (plain Kotlin, no Android). A local player's reports agree with each other to ~1 ms and are applied at once when 15 ms or more off (players report in bursts, then every second or two, so waiting for a second opinion leaves the lyrics off that long). A relayed session (`PLAYBACK_TYPE_REMOTE`, e.g. KDE Connect) sends stray reports, so its reports are votes: the clock moves once two 300 ms or more apart agree; jumps of 1 s or more and a song's first 3 s are taken at once. `LyricClockReplayTest` replays `SpicySync` logcat captures through it (`SYNC_REPLAY_LOGS`) and scores it against what the logging build did. Pause reports are not trusted (YouTube Music gives a sample up to ~1.3 s old and counts on from it after resuming, KDE Connect up to ~4 s): one behind the clock keeps the clock in place, and later reports are read as-is or offset by that gap, whichever agrees with the clock (`ClockCorrection.reportBiasMs`). The bias is kept only if the first report after the resume carries on from the pause report (within 50 ms: YouTube Music, ≤36 ms in the logs); Spotify and KDE Connect resume 71 ms-3 s past theirs. Before that check the bias locked Spotify a second ahead. Spotify's resume is wrong the other way: measured against the mixer's frame count (`dumpsys media.audio_flinger`, Server column of its track), its audio carries on from the pause but its reports after a resume run ahead, more the longer the pause (~150 ms after 0.5 s, ~850 ms after 5 s). So a local player's resume ahead of the paused clock (under `RESUME_JUMP_MAX_MS`, 3 s) leaves the clock in place and becomes a negative bias, kept through later pauses while the pause report agrees. Only a `STATE_PAUSED` report counts as a pause: the buffering report after our own seek is behind the clock (which started at the target) and used to be read as a stale pause, keeping the lyrics 150-250 ms ahead. Judge sync on the phone's own output: scrcpy's audio forwarding (remote submix) shifts latency on every stop. `AudioOutputProfiles` stores a lyric delay per output route (e.g. Bluetooth vs. speaker). Lyrics are keyed on title + artist only, because players fill metadata in across several updates. The playback clock follows the full track identity.
2. **Lyrics backend**: `lyrics/NextLyricsBackend.kt` wires the OkHttp/Retrofit client, the ~16 `RemoteLyricsProvider`s in `network/data/providers/`, source preferences (SharedPreferences), the per-track memory cache, a 3-day disk cache in `cacheDir/lyrics`, and fetch-ahead for queued tracks. **Bump `CACHE_VERSION` whenever matching, conversion, or TTML layout changes**, or stale cached lyrics will keep being served.
   The **Lyrics Manager** sits in front of all of it: `lyrics/LocalLyricsStore.kt` keeps imported TTML in `filesDir/local-lyrics` (keyed on the cleaned title + artist like the disk cache, with a cover thumbnail), and the view model's `loadLyrics` serves a "just once" upload (held until the song changes or Reset TTML) or a saved song before the cache and the sources. UI: `ui/lyricsmanager/LyricsManager.kt`, opened from Settings → This song or an optional floating button. The **lyrics folder** (`lyrics/LyricsFolder.kt`, matching in the Android-free `LyricsFolderIndex.kt`) is a folder the user links there through the system folder picker and that is read where it is: .ttml and .lrc files, subfolders included, named by their own tags (LRC `[ti:]`/`[ar:]`, AMLL `musicName`/`artists` meta) or "Artist - Title" file names, either order. Its listing is cached in `filesDir/lyrics-folder-index.json`, so a rescan (on link, the Rescan button, or the app coming back after 30 s) reads only new or changed files; when that changes the playing song's pick, it reloads. A folder file ranks after a saved song and before the cache. `LrcConverter` turns LRC into TTML (enhanced LRC stays word-timed); the upload screen takes .lrc through it too. The three cache actions in Settings → Advanced: all caches for this song (memory, disk, Genius romanization; not the Lyrics Manager), the whole disk cache (what's on screen stays), and this song's memory copy only. Their results show as toasts (`ui/components/SpicyToast.kt`, fed by `ExternalPlaybackViewModel.messages`).
3. **Source selection**: `network/data/RemoteLyricsSource.kt`. It asks the lead source first, then fans out to the rest in parallel. It reports the best result so far as results arrive (`onUpdate`), stops early once a good enough result is in, and applies `ProviderCooldownTracker` backoff. Quality ranking and diagnostics feed Settings → Advanced. Relays rank by where their lyrics came from (`rankByOrigin`): Spicy Lyrics and RMM Revival (a relay of Spicy Lyrics' API by Apple Music ID) put Apple Music lyrics in Apple Music's place and Spicy Lyrics syncs in Spicy Lyrics' place, keeping the user's order between them; while Spicy Lyrics has no real answer (e.g. no Spotify match), the walk waits for RMM Revival in case it brings the sync. RMM Revival labels Apple Music's lyrics `spicy_lyrics` too; only community syncs carry an uploader, so a Spicy-labelled answer without one ranks as Apple Music. lrc.red (`LrcRedLyricsProvider`) is a relay too: it serves Apple Music's TTML (word, line and unsynced) by ISRC with `itunes:` renamed `lrc:` and `iTunesMetadata` renamed `sourceMetadata`, which the provider renames back; its site search finds the ISRC and the Spotify matcher picks the hit. Every answer ranks in Apple Music's place, and while it is still out the walk counts it as a possible Apple Music answer. Providers that need a Spotify track ID (Spicy Lyrics) get one from `network/data/spotify/SpotifyTrackResolver` + `SpotifyTrackMatcher`, which deliberately refuse unsafe matches rather than substitute a different recording. The matching heuristics are considered settled, so avoid re-tuning them without cause.
   **Custom sources** (`network/data/providers/CustomLyricsProvider.kt`; UI `ui/settings/CustomSources.kt`): user-written GET templates, stored in `lyrics_sources` under `custom` (names and addresses only) with header values in the separate `custom_source_headers` store that backups skip. `NextLyricsBackend.rebuild()` swaps the provider set when they change; their IDs start `custom_`, they rank after every built-in source by default, and the cache key carries each one's `revision` so an edited address drops its cached picks. Analytics report them only as "Custom". Templates are checked with ICU in mind (escape both braces).
   **Blends** (`network/data/LyricsBlends.kt` + `network/data/blend/`): the best line-synced answer ranked above gets QQ/Kugou/NetEase word timing laid under its own words. Each blend ranks just above its highest donor and is built locally once those answers are in, so it makes no requests of its own. Off by default; toggled in Settings → Sources.
4. **Normalisation**: providers return a `RemoteLyricsPayload`. TTML is first-class, so word timing never gets flattened to LRC. `lyrics/RemoteLyricsAdapter.kt` turns TTML (via `lyrics/spicy/parser/TtmlLyricsParser`), LRC, or plain text into renderer `Line`/`Word` models, credits footer included.
5. **Rendering**: `lyrics/spicy/canvas/LyricsView.kt` is a single Compose `Canvas` driven by one `withFrameNanos` loop that reads `currentTimeMs()` outside composition. `LyricsLayoutCalculator` + `LineWrapper` handle layout, `LyricsAnimator` (springs / cubic splines) handles animation, and `ScrollPolicyController` + `ScrollManager` handle scrolling. `LyricsRenderer` draws. `LetterSynthesizer` makes per-letter emphasis for held words. `romanization/` covers the per-script romanizers: kuroshiro over kuromoji for Japanese (read by pronunciation, whole-line context), the `pinyin` package's dictionary for Chinese, aromanize's pronunciation rules for Korean. Greek and Cyrillic stay on our own readable tables. `HumanRomanization` + `network/data/providers/GeniusRomanizationSource` add Genius romanizations: looked up after the lyrics show, aligned to the lines through the machine romaji, and laid over the lines that match (Settings → Lyrics → Romanization). Backgrounds are picked in `ui/background/SpicySessionBackground.kt` by Settings → Appearance → Background Type: Default = `kawarp/` (the Kawarp port; `DynamicBackgroundRenderer` below Android 13), Legacy = `ui/background/LegacyBackground.kt`, Auto/Artist Header/Cover Art = `StillImageBackground` (filtered, 1.25x, slide change, blur setting), Color = `ColorBackground` (three shades of the cover's main colour from `CoverColors`, a local stand-in for Spotify's extracted colours). Refined Background (on by default) crossfades instead: a new picture, a new set of colours, or a new type fades in over the old one, which is dropped once it has settled (1.5 s); the image backgrounds overhang the page by twice the blur instead of the 1.25x crop, and Color uses the two deepest shades. With Animated Cover on, the Default background follows the cover's video (Settings → Background → Follow the Animated Cover): `ui/background/MotionFrames.kt` takes 64 px frames about 12 times a second and Kawarp eases each in (`VIDEO_SMOOTHING_MS`) instead of crossfading; debug builds log the cost under `adb logcat -s KawarpVideo`. Every view and sampler of one animated cover shares one player (`ui/nowplaying/MotionCoverStream.kt`): it decodes into a GL texture of its own and draws each frame into every TextureView showing it, since a player feeds only one surface. Kawarp's speed follows the song (`kawarp/BackgroundSpeed.kt`) from Spotify's audio analysis. Track extras (release year, artist header, audio analysis) come from `network/data/spotify/SpotifyTrackExtras.kt` over the anonymous web-player token (embed page, `queryNpvArtist`, spclient audio-analysis), with `ItunesReleaseYear` as the year's fallback; `SharedSpotify` holds the one token and match memory both they and the Spicy Lyrics source use. The view model looks them up only when `setTrackExtrasWanted` says the screen shows them.
   Refreshed Simple Lyrics optionally adds a held-letter curve and flame glow, fixed-width soft fill, breathing interlude dots, 0.62em line gaps and a staggered spring ripple instead of Smooth Scrolling.
6. **UI**: `MainActivity.kt` holds the lyrics screen, fullscreen (system bars hidden, content kept clear of the camera cutout): the header (`ui/nowplaying/`, the compact NowBar, which springs into a big-cover view from a floating button, a tap on the cover, or on its own without lyrics if set; the cover skips on a sideways drag and plays/pauses on a double tap), the loading skeleton (`ui/components/LyricsSkeleton.kt`), the lyrics, and `ui/controls/LyricsControls` at the bottom (floating ViewControls, the glass timeline and the playback row; any touch shows them, they fade after 3 s while playing). Landscape (window wider than tall; the activity handles rotation itself, no recreation) follows the full-page NowBar: `ui/nowplaying/LandscapeMetrics.kt` places the cover panel on one side (`AppSettings.panelSide`, swapped by a floating button, slides 0.4 s) and the lyrics box on the other, active line centred; all controls sit on the cover (`CoverControls` in `ui/controls/LyricsControls.kt`, shown by a touch on the cover only, cover darkened to 55%); expand moves the cover to the centre. With Cover stage on (Settings → Now Playing) over the Default background, `ui/nowplaying/CoverStage.kt` fills the panel's side edge to edge with the cover instead, feathered toward the lyrics and dimmed by a veil, and the song, the timeline and the transport dock along its bottom (`StageControls`); it fades over 0.9 s, the panel's cover dissolving into it, and a side swap slides it out and back in rather than across. What fills it follows Stage art (`StageArt` + `StageArtChoice` in `ui/nowplaying/StageArt.kt`, plain Kotlin), each choice falling back down the list: the song's Spotify Canvas, the record's animated loop (on the header's own stream, so only with Animated Cover on), the still cover. The canvas is a 9:16 MP4 found by Spotify track ID (the player's, the manual one, else the Spotify match) through a third-party service (`network/canvas/SpotifyCanvasFinder.kt`, base URL in one constant), memoised per ID on disk, misses included; any failure is no canvas. The view model looks it up only while the stage shows it (`setCanvasWanted`) and drops it in the same update as the song, so a late answer can't land on the next song; while it is out the still cover stays. It plays through the same shared player as the animated covers (`MotionCovers`, HLS or progressive by URL), cropped to fill. Kawarp's Follow the Animated Cover stays on the animated cover, never the canvas. With the header hidden, the lyrics span the page and the portrait bottom bar returns. The design tokens live in `ui/theme/SpicyTokens.kt` and the glass ViewControl look in `ui/components/GlassButton.kt`; new chrome should use those rather than Material defaults. Pop-ups are `SpicyModal`s: an optional `skeleton` shows while one opens (content builds after, then fades in), `onBack` puts a back arrow in its header, and with animations on the plate animates to its content's size. Quick settings (`ui/settings/QuickSettings.kt`) holds the queue (`Queue.kt`) and the Lyrics Manager (`LyricsManagerContent`) as screens of its own, sliding via `screenSlide`. Interface animations (transport glyphs in `ui/controls/TransportGlyphs.kt`, list motion, pop-up and Settings motion) all read `LocalUiAnimations`, which is the setting and not low performance mode. Settings (`ui/settings/`) opens over the blurred song background: a list of groups (This song, Lyrics, Appearance, Screen, Sync, Sources, Advanced), each on its own page, with a search box across all of them. Its rows, toggle, slider and source cards are in `ui/components/SettingsControls.kt`; back steps out a page at a time with Android's predictive back.
7. **Power**: the lyrics screen asks for a 60 Hz display mode (`setPreferredRefreshRate` in `MainActivity`; Settings → Screen → Smoother motion lifts it). Paused, `LyricsView`'s frame loop rests once nothing has changed for 30 frames (a drag or tap wakes it, a seek is seen within 150 ms) and the Kawarp background drops to 20 fps once slowed down. Nothing read during composition may change every frame: the touch watcher feeds the idle timer through a flow, and the timeline reads its position only where it draws.
8. **Updates**: `update/`. `Releases` (plain Kotlin, tested) reads the GitHub releases API for `spicylyrics/mobile` and picks the newest APK (pre-releases per Settings → Advanced → Updates, on by default while the app is 0.x). `UpdateViewModel` checks on launch at most every 6 h, downloads, checks the `.sha256` asset and that the APK is this package, then commits a `PackageInstaller` session; `UpdateInstallReceiver` opens Android's confirmation. Off in debug builds (a separate `.debug` app).
9. **Spotify Free**: `ExternalPlaybackViewModel` explains a refused seek, previous or skip (`PlayerLimit`) once per session, for `com.spotify.music` only: the action is missing from the session, or the command changes nothing within 1.5 s (seek) or 2.5 s (skip).
